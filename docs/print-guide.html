Print a full-size test model

Every part that isn't bought exports as an STL, already rotated with its best face on the bed. Print the lot to check that every component fits and every control reaches, before any timber is cut. After that, only the final parts need printing again.

Bed size

The top and bottom boards are 180mm long, which doesn't fit a 180×180 bed even diagonally. Any 220mm-class printer is fine: Ender 3, Prusa MK4, and every Bambu except the A1 mini. On a smaller bed, those two boards would need splitting.

Test-model stand-ins

Full-size stand-ins for the timber carcass, baffle, divider and rear panel, plus the brass grille. They exist to prove the fit. Any colour will do: 0.2mm layers, 3 walls, 15% infill. Glue the four carcass boards at the mitres with the divider in its grooves, exactly as the timber will be.

What the test model is for — check these before cutting timber

  1. Display window alignment. The active area's position on the glass isn't in Waveshare's data. The model assumes a 2.2mm margin on one side (GLASS_MARGIN_NEAR in cad/build_radio.py). Check it against your module, adjust, and reprint the bezel and baffle.
  2. Digit size from bed. The clock digits come out about 6.7mm tall. Stand the model on your nightstand and check you can read them from the pillow.
  3. Knob fit. It should push onto the D-shaft firmly and clear the nut. If it's loose, reduce the bore by 0.1mm and reprint.
  4. Button travel. Each cap should click its switch cleanly and not rub the deck.
  5. Driver screw holes. Mark them from the real driver and drill; they aren't in the model on purpose.
  6. Seal. With the rear panel screwed down, press the speaker cone gently: it should push back slowly, like a closed bottle, rather than moving freely.
